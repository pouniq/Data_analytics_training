BUSINESS SPECIFICATION FILE — SYNTHETIC DATASET GENERATION
Business: Kohannegar (Rare & Unique Bookstore)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
0. BUSINESS OVERVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Name: Kohannegar
Type: Retailer of unique / rare books and related art collectibles
Cities of operation: Rasht, Nowshahr, Bandar Anzali
Target audience: Men and women, age 30-50
Sales channels: "online", "in_person"
Main product lines: poetry books, historical books, paintings,
    photography books, single-artist collection books
Product price range: 1,000,000 to 250,000,000 (currency units, e.g. IRR)
Recurring promotion: a 15% discount is applied store-wide at the end of
    every 2nd calendar month (i.e. end of Feb, Apr, Jun, Aug, Oct, Dec)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. TABLE: categories  (~30 rows)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fields: id, title, parent_id

Rules:
- Generate 6 top-level (parent_id = NULL) categories:
    1. Poetry Books
    2. Historical Books
    3. Paintings
    4. Photography Books
    5. Artist Collection Books
    6. Rare Manuscripts
- Generate 3-5 sub-categories under each top-level category (parent_id =
  the top-level category's id), totalling ~24 sub-categories, for a grand
  total of ~30 rows. Examples:
    Poetry Books -> Classical Persian Poetry, Contemporary Poetry,
        Translated World Poetry, Illustrated Poetry Editions
    Historical Books -> Ancient Persia, Islamic Era History,
        Modern Iranian History, World History, Local/Regional History
    Paintings -> Miniature Reproductions, Oil Painting Books,
        Watercolor Collections, Calligraphy Art
    Photography Books -> Black & White Photography, Landscape Photography,
        Portrait Photography, Documentary Photography
    Artist Collection Books -> Single-Artist Retrospectives,
        Limited Edition Artist Sets, Signed Artist Collections
    Rare Manuscripts -> Antique Reprints, Facsimile Editions,
        Numbered Collector Editions
- Assign each category an approximate popularity weight (not stored as a
  DB column, but used internally by the generator to bias product sales
  volume): assign weights on a 1-10 scale as follows:
    Classical Persian Poetry: 9
    Illustrated Poetry Editions: 8
    Ancient Persia: 7
    Islamic Era History: 6
    Single-Artist Retrospectives: 8
    Limited Edition Artist Sets: 5
    Black & White Photography: 6
    Landscape Photography: 7
    Numbered Collector Editions: 4
    Calligraphy Art: 5
    (all remaining categories: default weight 5, +/-1 random jitter)
  Top-level categories inherit the average popularity weight of their
  sub-categories for reporting purposes.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. TABLE: products  (~200 rows)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fields: id, title, detail, price, category (category_id)

Rules:
- Distribute ~200 products across the ~24 sub-categories, roughly
  proportional to each sub-category's popularity weight (higher weight =
  more products, range 5-12 products per sub-category).
- Price range per product type (uniform-random within range, then rounded
  to nearest 10,000):
    Poetry Books:              1,000,000  -  15,000,000
    Historical Books:          2,000,000  -  30,000,000
    Paintings:                 5,000,000  -  120,000,000
    Photography Books:         3,000,000  -  40,000,000
    Artist Collection Books:  10,000,000  -  180,000,000
    Rare Manuscripts:         20,000,000  - 250,000,000
- title: generate as "<Adjective> <Noun/Theme> <Type>" combinations in
  English, e.g. "Golden Age Persian Verses", "Silk Road Chronicles",
  "Anzali Coastal Reflections" (photography), "Miniature Masters Vol. 3".
  Ensure titles are unique.
- detail: 1-3 sentence English description referencing the category theme.
- Sales-tier assignment (used to bias order_detail generation, not a DB
  column): assign each product to one tier:
    "bestseller"  : 10% of products  (~20 products) - appear in ~40% of
                    all order lines
    "mid_seller"  : 55% of products (~110 products) - appear in ~55% of
                    all order lines
    "low_seller"  : 35% of products  (~70 products) - appear in ~5% of
                    all order lines
  Bestsellers should be concentrated in high-popularity sub-categories
  (Classical Persian Poetry, Single-Artist Retrospectives, Landscape
  Photography). Low sellers concentrated in Numbered Collector Editions
  and Limited Edition Artist Sets (naturally rare/expensive items sell
  less often).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. TABLE: users  (~1500 rows)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fields: id, first_name, last_name, user_name, password, birth_date,
        city, register_date, disabled

Rules:
- Names: generate realistic English-transliterated Persian first/last
  names (e.g. "Ali Rezaei", "Sara Mohammadi") from a pool of ~60 first
  names and ~60 last names, combined randomly with de-duplication of
  user_name.
- user_name: lowercase first_name.last_name + optional 2-digit number to
  ensure uniqueness.
- password: generate a random 10-character alphanumeric placeholder
  string (this is synthetic data, not a real password).
- birth_date: age distribution centered on the 30-50 target audience but
  with a realistic spread:
    18-29 years old: 15%
    30-39 years old: 40%
    40-50 years old: 35%
    51-65 years old: 10%
- city distribution:
    Rasht:          55%
    Nowshahr:       25%
    Bandar Anzali:  20%
- register_date: random date between 2021-01-01 and 2026-08-01, weighted
  so that ~40% of registrations fall in the most recent 12 months
  (business growth trend). Distribute non-uniformly across months with a
  mild seasonal bump in autumn (Sep-Nov, back-to-school/reading season)
  and December (gift-buying season).
- disabled (active/inactive flag):
    Active (disabled = 0):   88%
    Inactive (disabled = 1): 12%
- Repeat-customer flag (internal use, not a DB column): mark ~35% of
  users as "repeat customers" who will be biased toward generating
  multiple orders (see Orders section); the remaining 65% are "one-time
  or rare" customers.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. TABLE: roles  (fixed, small)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fields: id, title
Generate exactly 3 rows:
    1. "admin"
    2. "staff"
    3. "customer"

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. TABLE: roles_user  (mapping)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fields: role_id, user_id
Rules:
- Every user gets exactly one "customer" role row.
- Additionally assign the "staff" role to 8-12 randomly chosen users.
- Additionally assign the "admin" role to 2-3 randomly chosen users
  (may overlap with staff).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. TABLE: orders  (~7000 rows)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fields: id, user_id, order_date, sales_channel, was_paid

Rules:
- user_id: must reference an existing users.id.
  - Repeat customers (35% of users, see above) should generate 3-15
    orders each.
  - Non-repeat customers generate 0-2 orders each.
  - This naturally produces the ~7000 total order count; adjust random
    draw counts so the total lands close to 7000.
- order_date: must be >= that user's register_date. Distribute orders
  across the last 12 months of the business's timeline (2025-09 through
  2026-08, or align with register_date range) following this monthly
  weighting (relative index, normalize to sum to 7000):
    Jan: 0.9x   Feb: 1.3x (pre-discount-end bump)   Mar: 0.8x
    Apr: 1.0x   May: 0.9x   Jun: 1.3x (pre-discount-end bump)
    Jul: 0.8x   Aug: 0.9x   Sep: 1.1x (back-to-reading season)
    Oct: 1.0x   Nov: 1.2x (pre-discount-end bump)   Dec: 1.6x
        (year-end gifting + discount month, highest volume)
  Best-selling months: December, June, February, November.
  Slowest months: March, July, May.
  Within each month, bias order_date toward the last 3-4 days of the
  month slightly more heavily for Feb/Apr/Jun/Aug/Oct/Dec (the discount
  months), reflecting customers timing purchases around the 15% discount.
- Day-of-week distribution: weekends in Iran are Thursday/Friday.
  Weight: Thursday 1.4x, Friday 1.6x, all other days 1.0x (baseline).
- sales_channel: "online" 65%, "in_person" 35%.
- was_paid: 92% "paid" (1), 8% "unpaid/pending" (0). Unpaid orders
  should NOT receive a corresponding payments row.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. TABLE: order_detail2  (~20000 rows)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fields: order_id, product_id, detail_id (row's own primary key / line id),
        quantity, price

Rules:
- Every order gets 1-6 line items, averaging ~2.85 items/order (so that
  7000 orders x ~2.85 ≈ 20000 rows). Distribution of items-per-order:
    1 item:  30%
    2 items: 30%
    3 items: 20%
    4 items: 12%
    5 items: 6%
    6 items: 2%
- product_id: MUST be selected only from existing products.id. Selection
  probability per line item follows the product's sales tier:
    bestseller products share  40% of total line-item picks
    mid_seller products share  55% of total line-item picks
    low_seller products share   5% of total line-item picks
  (i.e., sample tier first using these weights, then sample uniformly
  within the chosen tier's product pool)
- Seasonal affinity: Photography Books (esp. Landscape Photography,
  themed around the coastal cities Nowshahr/Bandar Anzali) sell ~1.5x
  more in Apr-Jun and Aug-Sep (spring/summer/tourist season). Poetry
  Books sell more evenly year-round but get a ~1.3x bump in Oct-Dec
  (Yalda/winter reading season). Historical & Rare Manuscript items are
  relatively flat year-round.
- quantity: 1 unit in 80% of line items, 2 units in 15%, 3+ units in 5%
  (max 5). Paintings and Rare Manuscripts should almost always be
  quantity = 1 (unique/rare items).
- price: copy the product's current unit price at time of the line item
  (do not invent a different price; this keeps order_detail2 consistent
  with products.price). If the order falls within a discount period
  (see Discounts section), this field may store the pre-discount unit
  price; discount effect is applied at the payments level.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. TABLE: payments  (~6000 rows)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fields: id, order_id, payment_code, payment_date, payment_type,
        total_price, discount_id, discount_price, total_amount

Rules:
- Exactly one payments row per order where orders.was_paid = 1 (this
  naturally yields ~6000-6440 rows given the 92% paid rate on 7000
  orders; adjust sampling so total lands close to 6000).
- order_id: must reference an existing paid order.
- payment_date: must be >= the order's order_date, offset by 0-3 days
  (uniform random), reflecting near-immediate payment confirmation.
- payment_type distribution:
    "card"            55%
    "cash"            25% (in_person orders only)
    "bank_transfer"   15%
    "online_gateway"  5%  (online orders only)
- payment_code: random 12-character alphanumeric synthetic transaction
  code, unique per row.
- total_price: sum of (quantity * price) across that order's
  order_detail2 rows.
- discount_id: NULL for ~65% of payments. For the remaining ~35%, assign
  an active discount_id whose active_date/expire_date window contains
  the order_date (see Discounts section). Orders placed in the last 3-4
  days of Feb/Apr/Jun/Aug/Oct/Dec should have discount_id populated at a
  much higher rate (~80%), consistent with the recurring 15%
  end-of-bimonth promotion.
- discount_price: if discount_id is NULL, = 0. Otherwise = total_price *
  discount.percentage (see Discounts table), rounded to nearest 1,000.
- total_amount: MUST always equal total_price - discount_price. This is
  a hard consistency rule enforced by the generator, not sampled
  independently.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. TABLE: discount  (~30 rows)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fields: id, title, discount_code, active_date, expire_date

Rules:
- Recurring bimonthly promotion (the core, most-used discount): generate
  one row for each end-of-bimonth window across the dataset's ~12-24
  month timeline (Feb, Apr, Jun, Aug, Oct, Dec of each covered year).
  Each row:
    title: "End of <Month> 15% Storewide Discount"
    percentage (implied by title, store as 15 if a numeric column is
        added, otherwise encode in title/detail): 15%
    active_date: the 25th of that month
    expire_date: the last day of that month
  This produces roughly 6-12 rows depending on how many years the
  dataset spans.
- Remaining ~18-24 rows: smaller occasion-based / category-specific
  discounts, varied:
    - "Yalda Night Poetry Special" (10-20%, active ~Dec 15-21)
    - "Nowruz New Year Gift Discount" (10-15%, active ~Mar 15-Apr 3)
    - "New Customer Welcome Discount" (5-10%, no fixed month, evergreen,
      i.e. active_date/expire_date spans the whole dataset range)
    - "Rare Manuscript Collector Days" (5-10%, short 3-5 day windows,
      2-3 times/year)
    - "Photography Book Summer Sale" (10-20%, active Jun-Aug)
    - A handful (5-8) of one-off small discounts (5-15%) scattered
      through the year with short 3-7 day validity windows, purely for
      dataset realism (not all discounts need to be heavily used).
- discount_code: unique 8-character alphanumeric code per row.
- percentage/value range across all discounts: 5% - 20% (store as either
  a dedicated numeric column if the generator adds one, or document it
  alongside the title for the Python script to parse).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. TABLE: basket_user (user_basket)  (~1000 rows)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fields: user_id, product_id, quantity

Rules:
- user_id: must reference an existing users.id. NOT every user has a
  basket entry. Users more likely to have an active basket:
    - Repeat customers (35% cohort): 3x more likely to have 1-4 basket
      items than non-repeat customers.
    - Users who registered within the last 60 days: 1.5x more likely
      (new users browsing before first purchase).
  Roughly 500-600 distinct users end up with basket rows, each holding
  1-3 items on average, totaling ~1000 rows.
- product_id: must reference an existing products.id. Products more
  likely to appear in baskets:
    - bestseller tier products: 50% of basket picks
    - mid_seller tier products: 40% of basket picks
    - low_seller tier products: 10% of basket picks (browsing curiosity
      on rare/expensive items even if rarely purchased)
- quantity: 1 unit in 85% of basket rows, 2 units in 12%, 3+ in 3%
  (max 4).

11. REALISTIC PATTERNS TO IMPLEMENT (minimum 10)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Seasonal sales increase: Dec, Jun, Feb, Nov are peak order months
   (see order_date weighting in Section 6).
2. Seasonal sales decrease: Mar, Jul, May are the slowest months.
3. Bestseller concentration: 10% of products ("bestseller" tier) account
   for ~40% of all order line items (Pareto-like skew).
4. Loyal/repeat customers: 35% of users generate the large majority
   (~85%+) of total orders (3-15 orders each vs 0-2 for the rest).
5. Seasonal product-category affinity: Photography books peak in
   spring/summer (coastal tourism season); Poetry books peak in
   autumn/winter (Yalda/reading season).
6. Discount-driven sales spikes: order volume and discount usage both
   rise sharply in the final days of Feb/Apr/Jun/Aug/Oct/Dec around the
   recurring 15% promotion.
7. New vs. returning customer behavior: newly registered users (<60
   days) are more likely to have items sitting in their basket without
   completing checkout; repeat/older customers convert baskets to orders
   faster and more often.
8. Order size variance: small orders (1 item) and larger orders (4-6
   items) coexist, with a realistic right-skewed distribution rather
   than a flat/uniform one (Section 7).
9. Price-tier driven quantity behavior: expensive/rare items (Paintings,
   Rare Manuscripts) are almost always purchased at quantity = 1, while
   inexpensive poetry books are more often bought in quantity 2-3
   (as gifts).
10. Payment-method / channel correlation: "cash" payments only occur on
    "in_person" orders; "online_gateway" payments only occur on "online"
    orders, reflecting realistic channel-payment coupling.
11. Registration growth trend: user registrations skew toward the more
    recent 12 months of the dataset timeline, reflecting business growth
    over time, with mild seasonal bumps in autumn and December.
12. Geographic weighting: Rasht (largest city among the three) supplies
    the majority (55%) of customers and, by extension, orders.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
12. DATA CONSISTENCY / REFERENTIAL INTEGRITY RULES (must always hold)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Every orders.user_id must exist in users.id.
- Every order_detail2.product_id must exist in products.id.
- Every products.category must exist in categories.id.
- Every order_detail2.order_id must exist in orders.id.
- Every payments.order_id must exist in orders.id, and that order's
  was_paid must be 1 (unpaid orders never get a payments row).
- Every payments.discount_id must exist in discount.id, OR be NULL.
- Every roles_user.user_id must exist in users.id.
- Every roles_user.role_id must exist in roles.id.
- Every basket_user.user_id must exist in users.id.
- Every basket_user.product_id must exist in products.id.
- All primary key ids (users, roles, categories, products, orders,
  order_detail2, payments, discount) must be unique, positive integers,
  generated sequentially or via a checked-unique random generator.
- orders.order_date must never be earlier than that order's
  users.register_date.
- payments.payment_date must never be earlier than that payment's
  orders.order_date.
- payments.total_amount must always equal
  payments.total_price - payments.discount_price (hard invariant,
  validated after generation, not just sampled independently).
- payments.discount_price must be 0 whenever payments.discount_id is
  NULL.
- All generated text fields (names, titles, details, cities, codes)
  must be in English only, to avoid MySQL charset/encoding issues on
  import.
